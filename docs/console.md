# The console

`apps/console/` is a client of the server, like any other: it never opens the store or the TOML. A new conversation starts, on a screen of CONVERSATIONS, by choosing whom it is with: a card for each agent of depth 0 not only in workflows, with its portrait (fetched by `apps/kit/client`'s `AgentImages`, which the studio shares); the conversation keeps the agent its first message named, and every message names it again. The `Omunculus` singleton holds the project as the window sees it and makes every request through `curl` on the project's local socket; the conversation's event stream is `curl` on its WebSocket, whose frames `EventStream` splits into events. It folds the events into threads: the main one, yours with the root agent, and one per work handed down, where its agent's runs show; a run at depth 0 belongs to the main thread, a deeper one to its work's. Each agent's turn, in a thread or in the inspector, shows its portrait, small, beside its name, or its initial in its colour while it has none (`Portrait`, as on the map's cards and in the inspector); yours shows none. What an agent says, a request's reason and a work's goal and notes are the kit's `Prose`, their web addresses links that open in the browser. A message carries the files picked under the composer with the kit's `FilePicker`, sent through `Http.post`; a file a message or an agent's `attach` carries shows under it as the kit's `FileChip`, which `Http.openAttachment` saves to the downloads directory and opens, as in the mailbox. A request's card (`RequestDecision`) says who asked, what for and who decides, you or an agent you can overrule; one the harness opened, passing on a question an agent left unanswered, leads with that question as its asker wrote it, under the asker's name, the harness's sentence in small print beneath, as in the mailbox; while it is pending it has its options as buttons, and so are those of every decision it gathers from below — an origin still waiting whose options are named, as an escalation keeps a decision's options on its origin — each under its part name and who asked it, a grant's scopes read for that origin's asker and work, an option of an origin given through the reply's `parts`, down to it (the kit's `Requests.choices` and `Requests.answer`, as in the mailbox); every option carries the words of its field: ANSWER replies with them alone, omitting `option`, DENY gives them as the reason of a no, and, when it waits on you, ESCALATE raises it with them as the reason through `POST …/requests/escalate`, as one part named after its asker. Requests and unread notes are not in threads: they hang from the work that made them on the orchestration map, a flowchart on the kit's `FlowCanvas`, which moves and zooms as the studio's flow does: you on top, a row per depth named at its left, each work a card with its agent's portrait, model, title, state and the tool it calls while it runs, laid out as a tree, each under the one it came from, parents centred over their children, running agents ringed and their links flowing, idle ones hollow; a work that goes through a workflow is a lane instead (`WorkflowLane`), as the studio's flow draws a workflow: a frame named after it with a card per stage, in order, each of the agent the setup gives that stage, the stage the work is at its live card, those it went through hollow and checked, those ahead hollow, showing who takes it; the lane is the work's node in the tree, its parent's link arriving at its frame and its children's leaving from the stage it is at. Picking a work opens its inspector, its portrait, for how long it has run and what it calls now, where it stands in its workflow (`WorkflowTrack`: the stages in order, those passed checked, the current lit), the work, and its runs, what it said and did; beside WORK, its other sections read the core's tools through `POST /v1/cli`: RUNS, the work's runs as `runs` sums up the project's last closed ones, matched by id with the snapshot's — how each ended, its calls, those that failed and what its model used and cost, a run still open saying so — with the runs, failed calls and cost in all above them, each opening call by call (`runs --run`, Esc back); CHANGES, the files its calls changed (`changes --work_id`) or, with DIFF, their unified diff, added and removed lines coloured, or why there is none, as the project not recording changes; PROMPT, the prompt its last run, or the run opened, was given (`prompt --run`), or that it recorded none. Each scrolls and copies what it shows. It reads the snapshot again after each event, and keeps every event as a line of a log too. The window opens on its start, clean: NEW CONVERSATION (N), CONVERSATIONS (H) and BOARD (B), and the project's name. Entered by one of them, its pages (`PagesView`) are in two levels, only those of the level on screen showing. The project's: CONVERSATIONS, the project's, filtered by what agents do there and what was first said, which it opens from, and BOARD, the works of every project the window's socket lists (`GET /v1/projects`), each read on its own socket through `GET /works` and kept live by its project stream (`GET /stream`) while the board is on screen. The board (`BoardView`) is a kanban, a column per state: open, waiting on a child or a request, broken (waiting with nothing named, for its parent to decide), done and cancelled, the latest changed first; each card (`BoardCard`) is the work first — its project, its title, in a workflow the stage it is at of how many, waiting what on, its conversation and the work it came from — then, small, its agent's portrait and name and its age, its border lit while an agent runs on it; BY STATE (S), BY AGENT (A) and BY PROJECT (P) cross the columns with a lane of each, chips keep one project, and `/` filters by title, project, conversation, workflow, stage or agent; the arrows move across and down the columns, and Enter or a click opens the work on a page of its own (`WorkView`), reached from the board as BACK shows, read as an issue: where it is — its project, its conversation, the work it came from, which opens — and its title on top; on the left its description, the goal it was given, what it asked and the notes it left (`WorkSummary`, as the map's inspector has them), and under them its RUNS, CHANGES and PROMPT; on the right its properties: its state, its project, its workflow with each stage beside the agent that takes it, those passed checked and the one it is at lit, or the agent it is with, the work it came from, or you through the conversation's agent, the works it handed down, each opening on its page, what comes next — the next stage or whom it reports to — where it works, and how long ago it began and last changed; its bar has REPLY (R) while it asked you something and OPEN IN MAP (O), its conversation's map with it picked; the window turns to the work's project first when it is another one (`Omunculus.switchProject`). A conversation's, in place of the project's once one is opened: CHAT, the chat with the root agent and its composer; MAP, the orchestration map; LOG, every event a row of a table that filters by text and opens an event whole; COST, what the conversation used, model calls, tokens and cost, in all and by agent (`CostView`). The masthead names the page: the project, then the conversation open and its page, or the page; as in the studio, the pages of the level are a carousel down the left edge (the kit's `TabRail`), the one on screen at its head beside the bar of its commands (the kit's `PageBar`), which ranks them — BACK apart, the page's main action solid (NEW CONVERSATION on CONVERSATIONS, REPLY where something waits on you, PENDING on MAP), the others outlined, BY STATE, BY AGENT and BY PROJECT joined as one control, and FILTER, COPY and FIT as icons at its end — carries the readout and shortens the commands' names when they do not fit: choosing another page of the level replaces the one on screen, while every move into another level, conversation or project is remembered, and BACK returns to exactly where it was, its page, project, conversation and the node picked, and from the first page entered to the start, the board keeping its grouping, filter and the card the keyboard was on; on CONVERSATIONS, NEW CONVERSATION (N); on CHAT, while a request waits on you REPLY (R), which brings the oldest into view and opens its decision (`RequestDialog`, the card over the page), while a note to you is unread MARK READ (M), which brings the oldest into view and marks it read, then NEW CONVERSATION (N); on MAP, FIT (F) and, while something waits on you, PENDING, which picks it; with a node picked, instead of FIT and PENDING, what applies to it: REPLY (R) while it has a pending request, the one waiting on you first, ESCALATE (E) while one waits on you, which opens the decision with Enter escalating, COMMENT (C) for a work, which puts the keyboard in its inspector's composer with Enter leaving a note (Esc back to writing), MARK READ (M) while it has notes unread, all of them, and WRITE (W), the composer writing to its agent; the in-place cards and the note's check still answer and mark one; on LOG, FILTER (`/`) and COPY, the event picked or else the rows shown; on COST, BACK; on BOARD, its groupings and FILTER (`/`). The digit each page shows, or Ctrl with it, chooses it, and a command's letter, or Alt with it, runs the command, a field being written keeping its digits and letters and the picker of a new conversation its digits; Ctrl+Tab and Ctrl+Shift+Tab turn the carousel, and the IPC's `start`, `page <name>` (`conversations`, `board` and, with a conversation open, `chat`, `map`, `log`, `cost`), `conversations`, `agents`, `log`, `board`, `open <id>`, `work <id>` (a work of the board, on its page), `newConversation` and `back` move it too. Esc first lets go of what the page holds — the map's inspector, the log's filter and the event picked, the board's filter — then goes back as BACK does. On the map, a card the keyboard reaches comes into view. Views only bind that singleton. The theme and the controls more than one app uses are in `apps/kit/ui`, a copy of eco's overlay, Omarchy's voice session app, and every request goes through `apps/kit/client`'s `Http`, which finds the scripts beside it; each app reaches the kit through its `kit` link to `apps/kit/`, since Quickshell imports only from within the folder it runs. It needs Quickshell, `curl` with WebSocket support and a Wayland session; `apps/kit/run console` takes the server registry and a project id, which may be left out when the registry holds one project; `mix omunculus.socket` resolves the project's socket, and the launcher passes both as `OMUNCULUS_SOCKET` and `OMUNCULUS_PROJECT`, as it does for every app.
